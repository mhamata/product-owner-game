Scenario 01

The Canadian Launch — Moomoo

Role: Senior Product Manager, Moomoo Canada
Product: Moomoo Canadian mobile trading app
Timeframe: 6 iterations (simulated 6-week quarter)
Difficulty: Intermediate–Advanced
Target skills: Regulated fintech PM · mobile product · international expansion · stakeholder navigation · social features · brokerage ops

Authored: 2026-04-19 · For PRAXIS interview-prep build · Mike Hamata / Moomoo Senior PM interview

1. Setup & Premise

You've just joined Moomoo Canada as Senior PM, 8 weeks in. Moomoo is a fast-growing, Hong Kong–listed mobile trading platform (parent: Futu Holdings) known for low-cost US equity access and strong social-investing features. You're responsible for the Canadian app experience, shipped to market 6 months ago. Canada is Moomoo's third international expansion after Singapore and Australia.

You inherited a roadmap from a departed PM, a 6-person Canadian squad (3 devs, 1 designer, 1 QA, yourself), a Chinese parent company with aggressive growth targets, and a regulatory environment (CIRO, provincial commissions) you must respect but the Chinese parent doesn't fully understand.

Your quarter-ending ask from HQ: "Hit 100,000 active Canadian retail traders by end of quarter with at least 40% mobile DAU/MAU ratio. Show us Canada can scale."

Current state (what's true at iteration 1)

  • ~22,000 active Canadian users (need to 5× in 6 weeks — unrealistic but you must show progress)
  • Product-market fit unclear — retention is poor after day 14
  • Wealthsimple and Questrade dominate Canadian retail, with brand trust Moomoo lacks
  • Moomoo's Canadian app is a thin localization of the US/HK apps — some features feel foreign
  • Canadian users want TFSA/RRSP support (critical local feature) — not prioritized by HQ
  • Social feed is active but largely cross-posted from US users; Canadian voice is quiet
  • Market-data costs are eating the P&L — Canadian users expect US data for free
  • Team morale is mixed after PM departure; devs feel whipsawed between HQ asks and local reality

Moomoo's structural constraints (these matter)

  • HQ decision speed is slow — platform changes need HK sign-off; local changes you can own
  • Compliance is non-negotiable — CIRO enforcement is strict; a social-feed feature that works in HK can get you fined in Canada
  • Mobile-first, always — 95% of Canadian sessions are mobile; desktop is an afterthought
  • Parent company prizes growth metrics — DAU, signups, volume — less interest in retention or NPS
  • "Pro trader" user archetype dominates HQ thinking — Canadian market is mostly beginners

7. Learning Goals for Mike (Senior PM interview prep)

This scenario is specifically designed to rehearse skills Moomoo's interview will test. Moomoo is a fast-moving, Chinese-parent mobile brokerage expanding internationally. Senior PM candidates will face questions on:

  • Stakeholder navigation — how do you manage HQ pressure while protecting local product quality?
  • Regulatory judgment — CIRO/compliance is non-negotiable; show you understand when to push back
  • Growth vs retention — HQ wants DAU; PMF needs retention; which do you pick and why?
  • Prioritization — with 14 customer asks and 5 tech investments and unlimited demand, what ships?
  • Team leadership — you're likely managing a local team with a global parent — boundary work
  • Product decisions under ambiguity — no playbook; write your own

Running this scenario 3× with different strategies (growth-first / compliance-first / retention-first) gives you three defendable narratives for behavioral questions like "Tell me about a time you had to push back on leadership."

8. Rehearsal Plan

  1. Playthrough 1: "The HQ Loyalist" — Accept every HQ ask. Observe what breaks. Document in retro.
  2. Playthrough 2: "The Local Product Leader" — Ship TFSA in iter 1. Push back on crypto. See how HQ trust evolves.
  3. Playthrough 3: "The Compliance-First PM" — Invest heavily in the compliance automation tech item first. Ship nothing customer-facing until iter 2.
  4. Review all 3 AI retrospectives side-by-side. Notice which patterns emerge. Write your 3 best-defendable decisions down as interview stories.
  5. Day-of: pick the strategy that best matches how Moomoo signals in the interview. If they love growth, emphasize playthrough 1. If they emphasize compliance, emphasize playthrough 3. Adapt.

Bottom line: this scenario is calibrated to the actual pressures a Senior PM at Moomoo Canada faces — rooted in your Questrade domain knowledge (CIRO, TFSA/RRSP, US options clearing, customer acquisition in Canada) and built to surface the specific tensions that make for strong interview narratives.
