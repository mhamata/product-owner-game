What you’re looking at

The turn arrives as people

Same engine decisions as today’s sim — routed through named characters with trust, moods, and memory. Maya’s pushback is the backlog trade-off; Dana’s ask is the stakeholder event. People carry the drama; the engine stays deterministic.

Telegraphed choices, real trade-offs

Every option shows its expected effects up front (the engine’s existing telegraphed-event pattern). One option is locked behind a skill — mastering Experiment Design literally adds a move to your game.

The rationale line is the Evidence Engine

One optional sentence per decision compounds into the Career File — the “tell me about a time…” record a junior PM can hand a hiring manager. This is the answer to “no one hires me without experience.”

The product is a place

Districts sized by investment, health bars, and visible tech debt (the red hatching in Checkout is the Sprint 3 shortcut). All of it renders from state the engine already tracks — this screen is visualization, not new simulation.

Fog of war: skills grant vision

The cohort panel is locked until the learner masters Cohort Analysis. Curriculum becomes the tech tree — you study because you want to see. The single strongest education-and-game fusion mechanic in the design.

Decisions annotated on charts

The amber dot on Activation marks the Sprint 5 pricing change. Cause and effect stay attributable across lag — the thing real PM dashboards make hardest to learn.

Board confidence is the fail state

Today’s sim can’t be lost. Seasons add stakes: drop below 30 and you’re fired — into a job market with offers matched to your demonstrated record. Getting fired and rebuilding is a better story than never failing.

The roster remembers

Each person keeps trust, an agenda, and a memory of what you did. The weekly 1:1 slot is a deliberately scarce repair tool — spending it is itself a prioritization decision.

The Career File is the product

Decision log, graded artifacts, incident record, interview stories — compiled automatically from play, exported for recruiters. The sim doesn’t just teach experience. It documents it.
