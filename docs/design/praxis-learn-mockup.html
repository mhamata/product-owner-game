    What you’re looking at

    One session, three acts, under six minutes

    The master plan’s daily loop, designed: two due-queue judgment cards, ONE scheduler-picked block targeting your weakest live competency, and the sim cliffhanger as the finale. The session always ends inside your story — that’s the retention engine.

    The scheduler picks, the mentor narrates

    The block choice is deterministic (competency strength + due reviews); the mentor’s one-liner is a cheap templated AI call that explains why. The LLM never picks exercise IDs — same cost architecture the plan already rules.

    Wrongness is never punished — absence is

    A missed card gets a one-line explanation and quietly joins the review deck, to return later in a different industry skin. Streaks and Cred reward showing up; the rating (elsewhere) only ever measures performances under constraint.

    The syllabus becomes a tech tree

    Every skill names the in-sim power it grants — Experiment Design is literally the locked option from Maya’s decision in the Sim concept. You stop studying because you should, and start because you want the move.

    Mastery decays; it never re-locks

    Ship/hold judgment has gone stale at 54% — amber, with a 90-second refresh in a different industry skin (spacing + variation). Re-locking mastered skills is motivationally toxic; rust you can polish in 90 seconds is a reason to come back.

    Locked ≠ hidden

    You can always read what a locked skill would give you. Visible-but-unreachable power is the oldest motivation loop in games — and here every unlock is also a real PM competency with a pass bar behind it.
