# Sim 2.0 & the learning layer — design ruling

*Approved by Mike 2026-07-12. This document extends [MASTER_PLAN.md](../MASTER_PLAN.md) Pillars 2–4 with a concrete design for the simulation and learning surfaces. It changes NO phase gates: Phase 1 (Interview Gym wedge) ships and is judged exactly as planned; the sim UI rebuild is the Phase 2 iOS flagship the master plan already names. Companion mockups (interactive, phone-first, light+dark): [praxis-sim2-mockup.html](praxis-sim2-mockup.html) · [praxis-learn-mockup.html](praxis-learn-mockup.html).*

---

## 1. The thesis

The target user's core pain — *"nobody will hire me because I have no experience"* — defines the sim's job: **manufacture defensible experience**, not just teach. Football Manager's hook is identity + consequences + emergent stories you retell; SimCity's is visible systems. Praxis wins when a junior PM can say in a real interview: *"I ran a product through 12 sprints; in sprint 4 I chose retention work over a sales-demanded feature, churn dropped 3 points, and here's the PRD I wrote and the decision log"* — and hand over a link. **The sim is a machine that generates "tell me about a time…" — with receipts.**

## 2. The five systems

### 2.1 The People System (Football Manager's soul)
Replace the aggregate team-morale blob with a **named roster** (eng lead, designer, data, sales, CEO/board; key customers later). Each person carries trust, mood, an agenda, and **memory** (the AI-native doc's "consequence flags"). Decisions arrive *through people*, not menus — "Maya says the deadline is impossible" is the same engine trade-off with emotional texture. Adds: 1:1s as micro-roleplays on a deliberately scarce weekly slot; the multi-party meeting (one Sonnet call, three personas) as the quarterly boss battle.

*Engine reality:* net-new per-entity state, but `customers`/`stakeholders` already model the per-entity pattern to clone. People's *voices* are Haiku-templated garnish; outcomes stay engine-computed.

### 2.2 The Living Product (SimCity's soul)
The product becomes a **place**: districts sized by investment, health bars, **visible tech-debt hatching** on neglected modules, users as flow. Metric charts carry **decision annotations** ("▲ you shipped onboarding v2 here") so lagged cause→effect stays attributable — the engine's event log already supports attribution. **Fog of war:** dashboard panels are locked until the learner masters the competency that reads them (topline DAU free; cohort curves require Cohort Analysis — then unlocked on every future run, permanently).

*Engine reality:* almost entirely visualization of state the engine already tracks (debt/reliability/investment per area). Fog-of-war gating calls the existing pure `isMastered()`/`competencyCoverage()` helpers.

### 2.3 The Season Structure (FM's calendar)
Sprints are matches; a quarter is a season; the QBR (final sprint) is the end-of-season review. Adds **board confidence** — the fail state today's sim lacks. Drop below the floor → **fired** → the **job market**: offers from fictional companies matched to the *demonstrated* record (level, scope, industry). This stitches the five standalone scenarios into one continuous career and is the diegetic form of the master plan's career ladder. Getting fired and rebuilding at a smaller company is deliberately a supported (and good) story.

### 2.4 The Evidence Engine (the "no experience" answer)
Every run compiles a **Career File** automatically: the decision log (with the optional one-line rationale captured at commit), graded artifacts, incident records, outcomes, and **interview stories** — mentor-drafted STAR stories from things the learner actually did in-run, with data attached. Deep integration: at pivotal moments the sim asks for the *real artifact* (write the launch PRD, graded by the calibrated grader) and **the grade modulates execution risk in-engine** (a vague PRD raises scope-creep event odds; the annotation says which section was vague). The Career File is the recruiter-facing export — the master plan's "inspectable work sample, credible at n=1," generated by play.

### 2.5 The Education Fusion (curriculum as tech tree)
- **Skills unlock capabilities**, not checkmarks: Experiment Design → A/B-test and painted-door moves in-sim; Cohort Analysis → dashboard panes; Stakeholder Negotiation → dialogue moves; Incident Command → war-room actions. Locked ≠ hidden: the learner can always read the power they're missing.
- **Sim events trigger learning:** first sev-1 offers the 90-second primer; sim moments feed the Leitner review deck via the existing (currently unused) `reviewStore.resurface()` hook.
- **Post-mortems close the loop:** a bad outcome maps to the competency that caused it; the next Standup's block targets that gap. Requires the shared learner model — the Supabase `competency_state` + `exercise_events` tables (already migrated) replace today's per-modality silo stores.
- **Modalities go diegetic:** critique = a colleague's flawed PRD in your inbox; dataread = your own dashboard acting weird; incident = a live sim event; teardown = "the board wants your read on Qwikster."
- **Mastery decays (FSRS, already ruled), never re-locks:** stale skills show as *rust* with a 90-second refresh in a different industry skin. Diegetic frame: staying sharp for next season.

## 3. The approved UI direction

**Hybrid:** inbox-of-people as the turn spine · living product map as the second screen · season/QBR wrapper. Tone: **calm professional game** — Linear-meets-FM, coach not theme park (master-plan risk #7: career fiction must not read cringe to senior users). One-handed portrait, 60–90s turns, per the Standup spec.

**Light and dark are both product requirements.** The mockups share one token system (~40 CSS variables); light is a designed palette (contrast-corrected accents), not an inversion. This token set seeds the production theme layer:

| Token | Dark | Light |
|---|---|---|
| ground / card / raised | `#10151d` / `#171e28` / `#1c2634` | `#f1f4f7` / `#ffffff` / `#ffffff` |
| line / line-strong | `#26303d` / `#34404e` | `#dfe5eb` / `#c9d3dc` |
| ink / body / dim / dimmer | `#e8edf3` / `#b8c2ce` / `#8a97a6` / `#5f6d7d` | `#17202b` / `#46545f` / `#64737f` / `#8b99a5` |
| accent (interactive) / on-accent | `#4fb3a3` / `#0c1116` | `#1f8577` / `#ffffff` |
| good / warn / crit | `#63b57f` / `#e1a23c` / `#d96c5f` | `#2b8f54` / `#a8720e` / `#bc4a3c` |

Type: system stack; `ui-monospace` + `tabular-nums` for every metric; 10–11px letterspaced uppercase labels. Semantic color is never the accent. Debt hatching = repeating diagonal gradient in warn/crit at low alpha.

**Screens demonstrated in the mockups:**
- *Sim — Standup (inbox):* messages from named people with trust bars; decision sheets with telegraphed effect chips (existing engine pattern); one skill-locked option per sheet where natural; optional one-line rationale → Career File; commit → cliffhanger overlay.
- *Sim — Product:* metric tiles with sparklines + decision-annotation dots; district map with health bars and debt hatching; one fog-of-war locked panel with its unlock path named.
- *Sim — Season:* sprint timeline, board-confidence meter with expectations and the firing floor, roster cards (trust, agenda, memory), Career File summary, locked job market.
- *Learn — Standup home:* mentor one-liner (templated AI narration; the deterministic scheduler picks, the LLM never selects exercise IDs), 3 acts with sequential gating, judgment cards with verdict stamps + one-line explanations, misses feed the review deck, Cred ticks, session ends in the sim cliffhanger.
- *Learn — Skills:* tech tree grouped by level; every node names its in-sim unlock; one rusty node with 90-second refresh; locked nodes readable with disabled CTAs.

## 4. Deliberate exclusions

- **No multiplayer/leagues at launch** (existing DAU-gate ruling stands). Determinism gives ghost runs + challenge links for free — asynchronous competition without social infrastructure.
- **No AI-simulated outcomes.** The deterministic core is the moat *and* the cost architecture (the learning loop never meters). AI is garnish: voices, narration, grading.
- **No open-world sandbox.** Authored scenarios + seeded variation beat procedural mush and stay calibratable.
- **No Phase 1 derailment.** Week-12 wedge gates decide everything, unchanged.

## 6. Engineering constraints (verified 2026-07-12; details in the session scout reports)

- Engine: pure seeded reducer (`app/src/engine/`, ~1,040 LOC, 38 tests), phases `planning → committed → review` via `step()`. Serializable state ⇒ save/resume, replays, "branch from sprint 4," ghost runs are cheap. `EventEffect` is an open discriminated union — relationship/board effects are one-case additions.
- Scenarios are pure data (5 exist; industries are zero-code reskins). New content ships without engine changes.
- **The current sim UI's 6-step flow is hardcoded and load-bearing** (`SimRunner.tsx` / `SIM_STEPS`). Sim 2.0 is a presentation-layer *rebuild*, not a restyle — budget accordingly. The engine/UI split means the rebuild does not touch the engine.
- All sim/learning stores are localStorage-only today; the slice-E sync layer + Phase-0 tables are the ready-made backend (sim keys just need allowlisting in `app/src/lib/sync/keys.ts`).
- Modality results live in silo stores; the shared learner model lands in `competency_state`/`exercise_events` (schema already deployed).
- `reviewStore.resurface()` exists, tested, unused — wire it first.
- Cross-modality gating helpers already exist and are pure: `isMastered()`, `competencyCoverage()`.

## 7. Open questions (decide before Phase 2 build)

1. Naming: "Career File" vs "Praxis Record" vs tying into the existing readiness report brand.
2. Board-confidence tuning: floor value, decay/recovery rates, and how loudly to telegraph "fired below 30" (mockup says it plainly).
3. Firing UX: how harsh is the moment, and does the job market soften or sharpen it? (Ruling intent: a story beat, not a punishment screen.)
4. Rationale capture friction: optional one-liner at commit (mockup) vs post-sprint batch prompt vs mentor-drafted with edit.
5. Whether the Career File export waits for server-side grade records (the deferred verifiable-URL ruling) or ships browser-side first.
